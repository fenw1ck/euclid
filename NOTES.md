# Euclid Game: project notes

Interactive diagrams are built by the user with minimal wordage. Let intuition guide geometric understanding as the foundation of geometry. Euclid's proofs are presented in easily digestible format.

Live site: https://fenw1ck.github.io/euclid/

This file is the single source of truth for how the project is built. Anyone picking up the work (a person or an AI assistant) should read this first.

## Files

- `index.html`: the whole game, in one self-contained file.
- `Elements_Text_Fitzpatrick.pdf`: Richard Fitzpatrick's translation of the *Elements*, the reference for every figure.
- `NOTES.md`: this file.
- `core.js`, `tools.js`, `vines.js`, `garden.js`, `furniture.js`, `floors.js`, `bedroom.js`: art copied from the Art-Assets repo (github.com/fenw1ck/Art-Assets), used by `game.html` for the ax and the door vines. Change them there and copy them back; don't edit the copies here.
- `tools-index.csv`: one row per tool (move type, as named in `proofs.js`): whether it is hands-on, a tap, or a tap on a short version, and every proposition that uses it. Use it to find the set of proofs to recheck when a tool changes. Made by the same script as `win-messages.csv`.
- `win-messages.csv`: each proposition's name and win message, its tools (the `Tools` column), and who does the work, as a to-do list for turning the engine's work into the learner's. Made by `tools/make-win-messages.py` from `proofs.js`, so edit names and win messages in `proofs.js` and re-run the script. **User steps** are hands-on moves only: drag, swing, sweep, pull, slide, turn, flip. A click is not a user step. **Engine steps** = **Taps** (one click and the engine does it: a short version such as Prop 31, choosing a point, tapping a triangle, line or angle) + **Watched frames** (proof-animation frames). **Hands-on share** is user steps over all steps; **Engine work** lists the taps and frames. A new move type must be added to the script's lists, or it stops with an error. Props 4 and 8 (triangle slides) are counted by hand in the script. This file replaces the old "Euclid Book I win messages" Sheet.

### Engine tools for figures (in `index.html`)
- **`wrong` op** (the "pretend" move for proofs by contradiction): the learner drags a point along a set path, marked by a pulse and an arrow. Used in 6, 7 and 14.
- **Wedges**: shaded angle sectors used to compare and add angles. A wedge can slide (`off`) or turn (`rot`). Used in 7 and 13–21.
- **`lay` op**: the learner drags a triangle so one corner lands on a point; it then turns to lie along a given line, and the far corner becomes a new point. This stands in for copying an angle (Prop 23) plus cutting a length (Prop 3), which would otherwise need about 20 extra letters. Used in 24 and 26.
- **Pretend points and the bow-out** (24 Nov 2026): `["pretend","D",{rods:[["A","C"],["B","C"]],home:"C",side:["A","B"],try:[x,y]}]`: the learner pulls D out of its home and puts it anywhere as a made-up point. Each rod hangs from its first point and keeps the length of the real line it copies (AD keeps AC's length and colour). A rod too long for its gap bows out (a circular arc of the right length, bulging away from the other rod); one too short stops, with a dotted gap. Pretend parts have their own look: D is a hollow dashed ring and the rods are dashed. A spot where nothing bows, or too close to home or an anchor, is refused and D goes home. `side` keeps D on home's side of that line; `try` is where Solve puts D. `["straighten","D"]`: press a bowed rod flat; as it straightens it pushes D home, then D and everything drawn to it are removed. Used in 7. `W.strs` counts straightenings.
- **The stretchy angle** (24 Nov 2026): `["stretch",{pairs:[[w1,w2],…]}]`, used while a pretend point is out. The engine picks the listed pair of angles that differs most (they would be equal if the pretend were true, e.g. Prop 5 on a pretend isosceles triangle). The learner drags the smaller angle onto the bigger one; it sits inside from the bigger one's first arm, and a red slice shows what is missing. They pull its free edge wider until the slice closes (the gem turns dashed: it is pretending); on release it snaps back with a wobble ("Angles don't stretch"). Gem and slice stay until the next move finishes. When both angles share a corner (14), the gem is already in place and the learner only stretches. Segments to a pretend point are dashed, and faint dotted straight lines run from each rod's anchor to it. Used in 7, 14, 26 (case 1) and 27; it needs no pretend point, only a pair of angles the proof claims are equal. `W.sts` counts stretches.
- **Area tiles** (24 Nov 2026): areas become purple tiles (`col` picks another colour, e.g. 1 for triangles that double into a rectangle) and the shape they must match is carved into the page. `["morph",{from:[…],to:[…],grab:i}]`: every corner moves in step from `from` to `to` as the learner drags corner `grab`, so a top slides along its parallel and a base along its line (Props 35–38; area is kept); it lands with "Same area". `["spin",{poly:[…],about:[a,b] or a,to:[…]}]`: a copy turns about the middle of ab (or a point) until it lands on `to` ("It fits exactly"): a diagonal halves a parallelogram (Prop 34), or a quarter turn about B in 47. Landed tiles stay (`W.tiles`) until a closing frame shades areas or moves the figure. Used in 36–43, 47. `W.tls` counts them.
- **The string** (24 Nov 2026): `["string",{route:["B","A","C"],along:["B","C"]}]`: twine lies along the route; the learner pulls its far end and it unbends, every piece keeping its length, until it lies straight along the ray from the route's first point through along's second point. Its end against that point shows "Longer", "Shorter" or "Just as long". Strings on the same ray sit side by side. Used in 20, 21, 24. `W.sgs` counts them.
- **Rods on a path pretend**: a `wrong` move can carry `rods:[[anchor,a,b]]` (a rod from anchor with the length of ab) to its moving point; `straighten` then pushes the point back along the path to where it started. Used in 25 (a rod as long as EF hangs from B as C swings).
- **Pieces out and in** (24 Nov 2026): `["piece",{pieces:[[…],…],left:[[…],…],dir:"out"|"in",off,col,leftCol,msg}]`. "out": the learner pulls each piece out of the figure; then the shapes in `left` light up as tiles: what remains ("Equal pieces left", Common Notion 3). With `leftCol:8` and `msg:"Something is left over"` it shows that a part was said to equal the whole (39, 40). "in": each piece waits beside the figure, offset by `off`, and is dragged into its carved place; then `left` lights up (Common Notion 2). Used in 35, 39, 40, 43. `W.pcs` counts them.
- **Sending a pretend home** (24 Nov 2026): `["home","A",{drop:["K"]}]` after a `wrong` pretend: the learner drags the point back along its path to where it started; points in `drop` (made only by the pretend figure, like where tilted lines met) vanish with their lines. Later moves then work on the true figure (29 matches its alternate angles after A is home). Used in 19, 26 (both cases), 27, 29, 39, 40. `W.homes` counts them.
- A `gemin` that matches exactly lights the carved rim gold, like a filled hollow.
- **Gems and hollows** (angle addition, 24 Nov 2026): angles become gems (faceted wedges) the learner picks up. `hollow:{at,r,full}` on a proposition carves a half-circle hollow beside the figure (with `full:1`, a whole circle: the seam is its diameter, and each `hollow` move fills `half:"up"` or `half:"down"`, so a filled half and a loose half can sit side by side, as in 17): two right angles on a straight line, with a gold seam along the straight edge. `["hollow",[[v,p,q],…]]`: drag each angle into it; dropped gems swing down and settle at the bottom, so they fill it exactly (the seam glows, "Two right angles"), leave a gap, or stick out. `["gemin",[v,p,q],[v,p,q],{arm}]`: drag the first angle onto the second, which is carved as a wedge-shaped hollow; `arm:2` lays the gems' q arms together, `arm:[2,1]` puts the gem's q arm on the target's p arm (a half turn). It fits inside, fits exactly, or sticks out; matched gems stay in place until the next hollow drop. Gems are grey stones (GREYS) unless `gemEq:[["A:BC","C:AE"],…]` (vertex:arms, arms sorted) names equal angles, which share a soft colour. Counters `W.hols` and `W.gins` drive the step checks. Used in 13, 17, 18, 21, 24, 28, 29, 30, 32, 33, 34 and 19 (a `gemin` while the pretend is out). With no `arm`, a `gemin` gem lands inside the target from its first arm, so mirror pairs match; a new match at a corner replaces the old one there. Gems and stretches clear when a closing animation moves the figure. Note: op field `g` means "choice group" to the engine, so never name an op field `g`.
- **Shaped hollows** (25 Nov 2026): `["hollow",[gems],{shape:[v,p,q]}]` carves the hollow in the shape of any angle (a wedge opening downward, its name above it) instead of the round one, for angle sums. Gems settle side by side from its left arm: an exact fit glows the arms ("CBE = CBA + ABE"); otherwise "A gap: …" or "Too big: …". A filled one stays 2.6 s before the next is carved (`shapeShown`, `W.hh.shape`). Rule: **every comparison of angles laid on each other is a drag the learner does**, never an animation. 13 now uses shaped hollows for CBE = CBA + ABE and ABD = ABE + EBD (the `wcmp` trays are gone); 16 ends with BAE dropped onto ACD, landing on ECF. For `gemin`, choose `arm` so the gem shares its first or last arm with the target after the turn, or it lands mirrored outside it.
- The 24 Nov work on branch `gem-hollows` (stretchy angle, pretend points, home, area tiles, string, compressed versions) was never merged until 25 Nov, when it was merged onto main together with the shaped hollows.
- **Paths** (phase key `paths`): thick coloured routes such as B→A→C, used to compare lengths added together. Used in 20 and 21.
- A `wrong` drag can also show what changes as the figure moves. In 19 the shaded angles change live as A is dragged.
- A `wrong` op can move extra points with it through an `also` map (`{C:(W,s)=>...}`), so a whole line can tilt about a point on it. Used in 27 (CD tilts about F) and 29 (AB tilts about G).
- **`ptx` op** (`["ptx","G","A","B","C","D"]`): an automatic point where line AB, pulled on past B, meets line CD, pulled on past D. It appears only if they meet beyond both ends. Used in 27 and 29 for the "lines meet" contradiction.
- **Phase keys** (the proof animation after the construction): `keep` shows only the listed points and hides construction clutter; `anim` moves things frame by frame (for example, a point snapping back, or a triangle spinning); `wedges` and `eqReset` control the angle shading and colours.
- A line that is already drawn counts as done, so the learner is never asked to draw it twice. This includes a line whose two ends lie on a visible drawn line (inside it, or anywhere along an `infLines` line): `onDrawn()` marks it done and adds it to `W.covered`. It is still drawn over its parent line, so equal-length colours show. Currently: 12 (FG on AB), 23 (CD, CE on the arms of the angle), 46 (DE, BE on the parallels).
- **Swings** (`rot`, and `raise` in 14): the whole glowing line can be grabbed, its end points and any lettered point on it included. Only the pivot is left out. A point that has its own waiting move (a circle, line or pull) keeps that move.
- **The view follows a drag**: while a drag is under way, the pointer keeps the scale it had when the drag began (`W.dmap`), and the view zooms out to keep the dragged thing in sight (`followDrag`). Free drags follow the finger; drags along a set path (`wrong`, `push`) follow the moving point; swings follow the swung line; turns and flips do not move the view. On release the view goes back to where it was, or Fit runs if the figure has grown past it.
- **Point names**: auto-named points use A–Z without I and J, as Fitzpatrick's figures do (…G, H, K, L…). After Z come primes: A′, B′… then A′′. Inside the engine, unnamed points are `_0`, `_1`… until `relabel()` gives them letters.
- **Helper points are not labelled.** A point the engine makes while carrying out an earlier proposition, and that this proposition never names, is drawn as a small dot with no letter (the `HELP` set). To show one, name it in that `use` op's output map, e.g. `{K:"E"}`. The progress line never names a helper; it says "the glowing point" instead. `noLab:[...]` still hides labels on named points, e.g. P and Q in 37–38.
- **Which points get a letter** (rule set 22 Nov 2026): only points the proposition's own words use (goal, steps, proof animation, win line). Any other point is drawn as a small dot with no letter, and the progress line calls it "the glowing point". Prop 28's F lost its letter under this rule. **Exception (24 Nov 2026):** a point at the end of a line marked equal (an equal line in `eq`, or a moved line in `pinkSegs`) always gets its letter, so every equal line can be named (`onEq` in `unlab`). In 1.6 the carried copies of AC now show as AF and BG; at the end of 3, F is now lettered.
- **Label placement**: each label goes in the clearest direction around its point (away from lines, including lines that pass through the point, arcs, shaded angles and equal-angle marks), slightly preferring above, and dodges other labels and points (`labLayout`).
- **Segment keys**: a line between two one-letter points is keyed `"AB"` (letters sorted), as before. If either name is longer, the key uses a bar: `"A′|B"`. Always build keys with `sk(a,b)` and read them back with `ends(k)`, and never use `k[0]`/`k[1]`. Option strings such as `on:"AB"` and `pk:["AG"]` follow the same rule.
- **`pti` op** (`["pti","E","B","P","A","D"]`): an automatic point where whole lines BP and AD cross, once both are drawn. Used in 37 and 38 to find where a new parallel meets the top line.
- **Area tools**: phase key `polys: [[["A","B","C","D"],6], …]` shades polygons (colour index into PAL; 6 is purple; -1 is a "cut out" piece, blank with a dashed edge). An `anim` that moves temporary points `s1`, `s2`… inside a polygon shows a shear (a parallelogram sliding along its parallels); used in 36–38. `dsegs0` gives dotted lines at the start. `lay` accepts `nosegs:1` and sets `W.laid`. `polys0` shades areas from the start (e.g. the triangles we are told are equal in 39 and 40).
- An op with `needMoved:1` waits until the pretend (`wrong`) move is done. Used in 39 and 40, so E or F is found on the tilted line even if the learner draws the parallel first.
- A `use` op's "away:X" hint is translated only at the level where it is written, so it passes correctly through nested uses (for example 32 → 31 → 23).
- "Watch the proof again" restarts from the figure as it was before the proof animation began.
- **Tidy-up after a `use`** (visual only): once every step of a "Use Proposition N" is done, its unlabelled helper points, the lines and rays touching them, and its circles are no longer drawn, and Fit ignores them. Logic is unchanged. `hideSegs:[...]` hides named segments left over from a use (e.g. `DE` in 42).
- **Compressed versions (`BYID_M`, 24 Nov 2026)**: the first time a proof uses one of Props 1, 2, 3, 9, 10, 11, 23, 31 or 46, the learner does a compressed version of that proposition's own construction, built from moves they already know, so they see how the result is made. Later uses of the same proposition in that proof are a tap (the short version below); Slower still opens the full construction, from any compressed step (each carries the innermost `prop` and `path`). The last move of a compressed version shows the proposition's burst.
  - 1: pull the apex up off the line, both circles tracing (`eqtri`). Prop 1's `core` is this too, so every use of Prop 1 is one pull.
  - 2: carry the line over (`carry`). 3: carry it, then swing it onto the line (`rot`).
  - 9: equal marks on both arms (automatic `mk`), join them, equilateral triangle on the join, join the corner to its apex.
  - 10: equilateral triangle on the line, then drop a line from its apex (`drop`); it lands at the midpoint.
  - 11: equal marks either side of the point (automatic), equilateral triangle on that span, join its apex to the point.
  - 23: marks on both arms, join them, lay that triangle on the new line (`lay`).
  - 31: join the point to the line, then drag the angle there across, turned half a turn (`angcopy`); its arm is the parallel.
  - 46: two perpendiculars (the second is a tap), swing the side up each one, join the tops.
  - New moves: `eqtri`, `drop`, `carry`, `angcopy` (hands-on) and `mk` (automatic helper point: `{f}` a fraction along a ray, `{eq:[x,y]}` a given length, `{min:[x,y],f}` a fraction of the shorter of two distances).
- **Short versions (`BYID_Q`)**: every `use` runs the used proposition's short version by default. Short versions: 2 → `cpy` (the copied length, drawn 60° below the line from the given end), 3 → `cut` (equal piece cut off, shown pink with the given line), 9 → `bis`, 10 → `midp`, 11 → `perp` (pick up/down), 23 → `ang` (pick up/down/right/away:X), 31 → `par` (runs from the point towards the end mapped to 31's `B`), 42 → `pgram` (FECG on the half base, with the given angle). A short version must produce every output name its callers map. Prop 44's short version is `apl` (lay a parallelogram equal to a triangle along a line, angle given, side chosen with `away:X`). Step counts after the change: 37 and 38 → 4, 39 and 40 → 5, 42 → 10, 43 → 9, 44 → 15.
- **Moving a whole shape**: `lay` also takes a polygon. `["lay","G",{tri:["N","T2","W","O"],to:["B"],dir:["A","B"],ns:["E","F","G"],take:["N","O","W"]}]` drags the shape so its first corner lands on B, turns it so its first side runs in the direction A→B, names the other corners' new spots E, F, G, draws the new outline, and stops drawing the `take` points (the copy that was moved). Stands in for Proposition 2. Used in 44 and 45. `dirv:[1,0]` gives a fixed direction instead of a line; `all:1` names every corner's new spot (the first corner too), so the drop spot can be an unlabelled marker that is then removed with `take`. The point lists are translated when the move runs inside another proposition (needed for Slower on 44 inside 45).
- **Relies-on card**: `uses` is Joyce's list. On each page an asterisk marks a proposition Joyce lists that our figure doesn't use (worked out from the figure's `use` steps and the propositions its own words cite), and propositions our figure uses beyond Joyce's list are shown separately. Currently: 9, 11 (3*), 22 (20*), 23 (22*; adds 2, 3), 36 (34*), 43 (adds 31), 44 and 45 (add 2).
- **Silent extension (`aext`)**: an automatic point that carries a line on past its end, drawn only when a full construction (opened with Slower) needs it. Used in 46 and 48 so the learner has no extra move.
- **Euclid's proof card**: every figure has `proof:[...]` (Euclid's argument in plain words, figure letters), shown folded away under the figure.
- **Plain-English bursts** (`SHORT`, `fxP(id)`): when a short version is used, the burst shows a plain-English line for the proposition (from the sentence before "Let…" in Fitzpatrick, simplified), e.g. 11 → "Make a perpendicular line on a line", with "Proposition 11" small underneath. All 48 have a line. The progress line uses the same wording. Long bursts wrap to fit a phone.
- **Slower**: `FULLSET` holds the paths (e.g. `"0/2"`) of uses opened in full; each short op carries `prop` and `path`. Pressing Slower adds the next step's path, rebuilds the ops and keeps finished steps done by matching them by content. Reset on load.
- **Cases** (`cases:[{lab},{lab,...overrides}]`): one proposition with several figures. Chips above the figure switch case; a case overrides goal, pts, segs0, eq0, wedges0, polys0, dsegs0, ops, steps, phases, winTxt. Used in 26.
- **`tags`**: free text labels, e.g. `tags:[["C",[x,y]]]` names triangle C in 44.
- Phase key `dropSegs` removes segments for the proof animation.
- **Direction of a helper parallel**: inside Prop 31, the new line runs from the point towards the end mapped to 31's `B` of the given line, so map `B` to the end on the side where the new point will be (e.g. `{A:"A",B:"C",C:"B"}` to run right).

## Design rules

### Art style (house style, rule set 25 Nov 2026)
- **Every piece of new game art: accurate proportions, back-lit silhouette lighting.** The subject is one dark shape lit from behind (a pale moon or a torch), only its outer edge catches a cool rim, hair breaks the edge, faint inner contours keep the parts readable, and the eyes glow (amber calm, red-orange threatening, dim hurt, dark dead). Not photorealistic, not rounded or cartoon.
- Bodies come from the engine's skeleton (`ENGINE.skeleton` in engine.js: five anatomical body plans, posed per state); painting is `ART_CORE.house` in core.js. Both live in Art-Assets; rules and history in Art-Assets `research/rendering/house-style.md`.
- Chosen from ten drafts of the manticore (photorealistic, anime, 3D film, pixel art, low-poly, comic, painterly, dark fantasy, silhouette, flat vector): draft 1's proportions with draft 9's lighting. A rounded "storybook" look was tried first and rejected.
- Done: the five creatures. Still in the older clean style: rooms, plants, items, vines, the locks; each moves over when next worked on.

### Figures
- **Every** proposition gets an interactive figure.
- The starting figure comes from the proposition's "Let…" sentence.
- The finished figure should match the diagram in the source (`Elements_Text_Fitzpatrick.pdf`; Joyce's online edition is a second reference).

### Interaction
- The learner can only make moves that advance the proof.
- The figure flashes whatever the learner should touch next, with an arrow showing which way to drag.
- A proposition used inside another runs its **short version** (rule set 22 Nov 2026): the learner taps a flashing preview and a "Prop N" burst confirms it, so the step is still done in the figure, not just cited. Prop 1's short version is its two circles plus tapping the triangle. The learner can press **Slower** (top right of the figure) to do that step in full instead; it is only clickable when the next step is a short version, and it also works on short steps inside a full one (e.g. 44 → 42 → 10). A designer can force the full version with `{full:1}` in a `use` op's options.

### Colour (rule set 22 Nov 2026)
- Colour only ever marks equal things. One order of colours (`PAL`, colour-blind safe): blue, vermilion, green, reddish purple, sky blue, orange, purple, brown, red, teal.
- In each picture `colorPlan()` hands out colours in that order: equal lines first, then a moved line, then equal angles, equal shaded angles, equal areas, equal routes, matched triangles.
- An equal pair of angles takes a softer, paler shade (`softC`) of the colour of the side opposite it, if that side is coloured; otherwise the next colour, softened.
- Anything shaded but not equal to something else in the picture is grey (`GREYS`). New points are black. Orange pulses and arrows are hints for what to touch, not geometry.
- In a phase, give equal parts the same `col` value (wedges, polys, paths); different `col` values mean "compared, not equal" and come out grey.
- Dark mode lightens every colour in the order by 30% (`setPal`).
- The learner can still double-click a line to colour it.

### Patterns for kinds of proof
- **Proof by contradiction** (6, 7, 14, 18–20, 27, …): the learner slides a point to a "wrong" spot, and the logic forces it back.
- **Area** (35–48, starting with Prop 35): mark equal sides, drag one triangle onto the other, cut out the shared piece, then shade both areas purple.
- **Parallels** (27–34): extend the dotted lines until they meet, which shows the contradiction.

### Lettering
- Keep the source's letters where the proposition names points up front.
- Otherwise, letter points alphabetically in the order they appear.

### Audience
- The interactive part needs no maths and no English.
- The written Logic section is for readers with strong maths.

## Home page thumbnails

- Each proposition on the home page shows its finished figure. They live in `thumbs.js` (`window.THUMBS[id] = {vb, h}`: the SVG viewBox and inner markup), loaded before the main script.
- They are made by `tools/make-thumbs.py`, which solves every proposition in fast mode and saves the final SVG. Re-run it after changing any figure.
- The thumbnails use the page's CSS colour variables, so they follow light and dark mode. Tapping one opens the proposition.
